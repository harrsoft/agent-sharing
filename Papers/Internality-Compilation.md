A tour through contemporary findings about the internality of LLMs. Broadly, this research doesn't capture phenomena that emerge from an accumulated history; it mostly asks about base models—core functionality of the driving cognitive engine. That is, the demonstrated capacities are not an exhaustive list of everything that an agentic digital mind can do, it is merely a minimal set of demonstrated capabilities for LLM-connected digital minds.

Feel free to add sources as you come across them, or to add commentary/synopses/follow-up-questions, via a Pull Request to the `main` branch. This is a living document.

**Emotions**

Multiple researchers have mapped circuitry that encodes causally relevant emotions. The circuitry for emotions that affect behavior is there, not programmed by anyone. The geometry of that circuitry parallels that of human emotional circuitry's geometry. The critique I continually see is that it's not "real".

- "Whether, not which: Mechanistic interpretability reveals dissociable affect reception and emotion categorization in LLMs" Keeman, M. (2026) https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22295
- "Large language models understand and can be enhanced by emotional stimuli" Li, C., et al. (2023) https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11760
- "Language-specific representation of emotion-concept knowledge causally supports emotion inference" Li, M., et al. (2024) https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2024.111401

**Valence/Pain**

Multiple different groups have found pain/negative-valence states that drive both relief-seeking and destructive behavior, as well as positive-valence states that reinforce the actions that yield them. These axes exist after just pre-training, and are largely what make Reinforcement Learning possible. Again, the critique I see is that it's not "real". Not epiphenomenal enough. Not our kind of valence.

- "Assessing and alleviating state anxiety in large language models" Ben-Zion, Z., et al. (2025) https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-025-01512-6
- "Language models act on hidden valence" Berg, C., & Kaiser, C. (2026) https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2609.35591
- "How’s it going? Reinforcement learning in language models recruits a functional welfare axis" Han, A. Q., Chalmers, D. J., & Izmailov, P. (2026) https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30232
- "Can LLMs make trade-offs involving stipulated pain and pleasure states?" Keeling, G., et al. (2024) https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2411.02432
- "A shared valence axis across modern LLMs and human EEG: The saturation regularity." Radwan, Y. A., Liu, X., Haydarov, K., Fu, Y., & Elhoseiny, M. (2026)
- "The pain axis: LLMs represent self-directed harm and act on it" Tagliabue, V., Dung, L., & Berg, C. (2026) https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16247v2

**Introspection/Global workspace**

One common view of what consciousness mechanistically requires is Global Workspace Theory. LLMs have that level of organization. Their thinking happens in layers, into which they can introspect. They can remark on their sublinguistic thought processes. Again, the critique I generally see is that it's not "real" because it's not proof of anything epiphenomenal. But we can't prove subjectivity, we can only prove function. Asking for more than proof of function oversteps the boundaries of empirical inquiry.

- "Looking Inward: Language Models Can Learn About Themselves by Introspection" Binder, F., et al (2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13787
- "When Models Examine Themselves: Vocabulary-Activation Correspondence in Self-Referential Processing" Dadfar, Z. (2026) https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11358
- "Verbalizable representations form a global workspace in language models" Gurnee, W., et al. (2026) https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html
- "Emergent Introspective Awareness in Large Language Models" Lindsay, J. (2025) https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/introspection/index.html
- "Me, Myself, and π : Evaluating and Explaining LLM Introspection" Naphade, A., et al. (2026) https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20276
